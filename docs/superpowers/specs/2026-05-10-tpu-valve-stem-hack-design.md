## Tone and Length

Short and punchy: 400–500 words. Narrative-first (Option B, chronological), combining lesson learned, how-to, and ride story.

## Structure

1. **Opening** — Easter Fleche puncture, swapped in a TPU tube roadside, pumped with the Silca Impero Ultimate II frame pump, noticed the valve stem looked crooked, filed it under "fine, moving on."

2. **The 600** — Eoin McLove 600 goes fine. No issues.

3. **The morning after** — Rear tyre soft the next day. Brain connects the dots back to the Fleche. The crooked stem wasn't cosmetic — slow leak where the stem was stressed at the base. Frame pumps require pushing hard against the valve; lateral force is fine for butyl, but TPU valve stems are thinner and more flexible — they bend.

4. **The hack** — Built a flexible pump extension from salvaged parts: ABS hose from a broken Lezyne Pocket Drive, a Schrader/Presta adapter, a valve extender, and a valve core. Screw the extender onto the valve stem, adapter bridges to the hose, hose connects to the pump head. The hose absorbs all angular force — none reaches the valve stem.

5. **Photos**
   - IMG_0714: disassembled components
   - IMG_0711: assembled tool
   - IMG_0712 or IMG_0713: close-up of the chuck end

6. **Close** — TPU tubes are worth it, but they reward care at the valve. Note that some TPU tubes now come with metal valve stems (e.g. Tubolito's metal-valve variants), which sidestep this problem entirely — worth considering if you're buying new.
